# Review of 0.31.0.0

Version: 1.0 / 2026-08-14
Baseline: `main` at 0.30.0.0, `make verify` green before any change was made,
373 tests on CPython 3.12.3. Nothing in this release changes the package. Two
checks were widened, two documents gained content that was learned by running
the system rather than by reasoning about it, and one finding is recorded
without being fixed because fixing it is not this reviewer's to do.

---

## What this release is

Not a feature. It is the release where two claims about the gate were checked
against the gate and one of them was false.

### 1. The egress lint's scope had stopped covering its own promise

`docs/DEPLOYMENT.md` section 2 ends the egress inventory with a sentence saying
that all reach lives in `mavo/transport.py`, that a lint fails the build if a
second module acquires it, and that this is "what makes the table above
checkable rather than aspirational". The lint is
`network_reach_is_one_file` in `tests/lint_limitations.py`. It iterated
`_package_sources()`, which is `mavo/**/*.py`.

`tools/` was never in scope. The `Makefile` argues in its own comment that
`tools/` is inside the net, on the grounds that half of STATUS.json's measured
numbers are produced there and that the mutation runner is itself part of the
gate. Both statements are in the repository; they had simply never been read
against each other.

**Nothing had slipped through.** No tool imports a network client, which was
checked before the change. The finding is not a leak but a **scope that is
narrower than the claim resting on it**, which is a failure with no symptom
until the day something does slip. The trigger for noticing was T42's sampler:
the first tool whose entire purpose is to reach a second destination.

Widened to the package plus `tools/`, and verified red on a planted
`import urllib.request` under `tools/` and a planted `import socket` under
`mavo/`, green with neither.

**Class.** A rule with a gate check whose coverage silently stopped matching
the rule. This is distinct from "a rule without a gate check is a preference"
and worth its own name, because the existing heuristic does not fire: the rule
had a check, the check passed, and it was auditing a subset. The same shape
turned up the same week in the consumer repository, where a locale completeness
check covered 22 of 83 string keys while its documentation claimed all of them.

### 2. `decisions_recorded` agreed only with a copy of itself

`defects_logged` has been derived from the F-entries in `docs/METHODOLOGY.md`
since 0.6.0.0 and cross-checked against the README badge, on the reasoning
recorded there: a hand-typed count drifts on exactly the release that adds
entries. Its sibling `decisions_recorded` was compared against a README row and
against nothing else. Two numbers typed by hand, agreeing with each other.

Both said 27. `docs/DECISIONS.md` held 25 entries.

`check_decision_count_is_derived_from_the_log` now counts the log, and also
resolves every `D-NNN` cited anywhere in `docs/`, `README.md` and `TODO.md`
against the entries that exist.

### 3. The finding this release does not fix

The citation check immediately produced one: **`D-025` is cited and has no
entry.** `docs/WEBAPP.md` gives it as the reason publication went ahead without
T6, the legal position, and the sentence doing the citing says the reason "is
in the decision entry" and that "the decision is written down with its date".
There is no entry. `D-023` is absent too, but nothing cites it.

Of every claim in this repository, this is the one where "it is written down"
carries the most weight, and it is the one where the record is missing. Whether
the entry was drafted and lost, renumbered, or never written is not
reconstructible from the tree, so it is not reconstructed here. `D-025` is
named in `CITED_WITHOUT_AN_ENTRY` with a check that fails if the entry appears
(remove the tolerance) or if the citation disappears (remove it too), so the
item cannot rot in either direction while it waits for the operator.

The count is now pinned at 26, which is what the log holds.

## What else landed

**D-027 closes its outstanding measurement.** The entry said plainly that its
margin was an estimate until the host had run one uninterrupted hour with
`AccuracySec=1s`. It has: 107 intervals, all between 31 and 36 seconds, mean
33.06 against a nominal 30 plus 5 of jitter, where the same timer produced 33
to 60 seconds before. The distribution is flat across 31 to 35, which is the
shape the jitter setting predicts. The hypothesis written into the drop-in -
that `AccuracySec` rather than run duration dominated - held; had the tail near
50 seconds survived, the mechanism in that entry would have needed rewriting
rather than closing.

Two facts came free from the same reading: 108 polls against 3600/33.06 = 108.9
means the hour has no missing cycle, and no interval above 36 seconds means no
poll in it hit a timeout.

**D-028, the ADS-B sampler.** Collection only. D-019 governs publication and is
untouched by it. The load-bearing part is not the cadence but the attempt log:
one row per poll, with a null result count for a failure and zero for an empty
response, because a schema that cannot represent that difference collapses it
at the first timeout. The entry also records that the sampler's box is not
D-019's box, so a later reader does not merge two datasets answering two
questions.

**`docs/FEED-SPEC.md` 1.5.** Section 4a gains properties nine and ten. Nine is
the consumer-side half of the heartbeat and is the most general thing in the
document: any consumer of any feed without a heartbeat owes its readers an
attempt log, and it costs one table. Ten is that a metered feed must state its
meter, because a consumer that cannot see its remaining allowance cannot
distinguish its own exhaustion from the publisher's silence. Both were learned
by building the sampler, which is why they arrive now rather than at 1.4.

Section 3's first property also gains a note: consuming an interface under a
revocable agreement showed that an application process makes **reproducibility
a property of the interface rather than of the consumer's diligence**. A reader
cannot re-run a measurement resting on an agreement they were not party to.

## What was deliberately not done

**No new defect number.** The two findings above are gate-scope findings rather
than behaviour defects: no output was ever wrong. Recording them as F-entries
would inflate a count whose meaning is "a thing this system got wrong for a
reader". They are in this review and in the changelog, which is where a
tightened check belongs.

**The sampler is not in this tree.** It runs from `/opt/mavo-adsb` on `vm-mavo`
with its own unit, user and store, so a fault in it cannot reach the collector
this repository is responsible for. When it moves in, the widened lint stops it
until it routes through `mavo/transport.py`, which is the intended outcome
rather than an obstacle. T42's acceptance already keeps its snapshots outside
the tree; the sampler follows them.

**No claim that the credit budget is measured.** D-028's arithmetic assumes one
credit per call against a 4,000-per-day allowance. Whether the endpoint returns
`x-rate-limit-remaining` has not been read from a response. Until it has, that
arithmetic is `[inference]` and the sixty-second interval is defended by
resolution alone, which is stated in the entry and in T42 rather than glossed.

## Gate

`make verify` green on CPython 3.12.3: 373 tests, coverage above the floor,
ruff and mypy clean over the package and `tools/`, and every document audit
passing. The two widened checks were each verified red against a planted
violation before being called live, on the standing rule that a check never
observed failing is not evidence.
