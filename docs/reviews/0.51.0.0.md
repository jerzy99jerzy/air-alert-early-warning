# Review 0.51.0.0: the outward documents were the unguarded ones

Version: 1.0 / 2026-08-31

The sequel to 0.50.0.0, and it is a sequel rather than a new subject. That
review was titled *the gates were counted, and one of them was counting
nothing*. It counted sixteen checks, found F139, built `figures.py`, and did
not notice that `brief_check.py` was the check counting nothing.

Opened, like the last one, on a judgement rather than a defect report: that the
documents had drifted and that several conclusions in them were stated more
precisely than the evidence allowed. Both halves held. The first was worse than
the judgement guessed and the second was smaller.

## 1. What the drift actually was

Four figures, seventeen releases, in the two documents an outside reader
reaches first. 87 defects against 118, 31 decisions against 45, 410 tests
against 642, coverage 96.61% against 95.42%.

Where they sat matters more than what they were. All four are in the section
whose entire content is the claim that the figures in this repository are
guarded, and the last question in the same document asked *which figures here
are enforced mechanically* and answered *every measured one*. That answer was
false at the moment it was written, and it was false about the paragraph four
lines above it.

The header line read `Measured: 2026-08-31, against STATUS.json at 0.50.0.0`.
It was bumped at 0.50.0.0, in this repository, by this author, in the pass that
rewrote the prose around those figures for the source switchover. The prose
moved. Nothing recomputed the figures. The line said something had.

**That is F139's mechanism arriving one release after F139 was written about
it**, which is the finding of this review rather than the defect. A figure
maintained by ceremony looks maintained, and writing an entry about ceremony
does not immunise the next ceremony.

## 2. Why three guards did not see it

`docs_audit.py` never opens either brief; it reads eleven documents and neither
of these is among them. `figures.py` regenerates the badges and the numbers
table in the README and stops there. `brief_check.py` reads both files, and
compared only whole numbers of four digits or more.

Every one of the four stale figures is under a thousand.

The narrowing was deliberate and documented: Polish writes a decimal comma and
English a decimal point, so `0,04` and `0.04` are the same figure in two
unmatchable spellings, and the module refused to carry a locale table nobody
would maintain. The reasoning is sound for comparing the briefs *to each
other*. It was applied to comparing them *to the pins*, where the two spellings
never meet, and there it buys nothing at all.

**And the module named the pin it was not checking.** `defects_logged` sat in
`PINNED` from the day the file was written, with a `value < 1000` guard three
lines below skipping every value that field will ever hold. Written, listed,
described in the docstring, and inert for its whole life. Class 2, the F38 to
F45 shape, in the module whose job is to catch exactly this in documents.

## 3. The fourth site, and it had written its own obituary

`README.md`, under *Measured claims*, whose first line is *a number appears in
this documentation only when the code produced it*, carried 471 tests and
96.84% coverage. Beside them, in the same bullet, a sentence stating that
`docs_audit` checks the badges and the table and **still does not check this
list**, so the correction made by hand at 0.33.0.2 and the update at 0.34.0.0
could both be missed the same way next time.

Sixteen releases later it had been. The prediction was accurate, dated,
committed, and worth nothing, because a risk written down and left unguarded is
the shape this repository already logs about itself as F72 and F139. This is
the third costume.

## 4. The fifth, found while repairing the fourth

The defect index in `docs/METHODOLOGY.md` ended at F99 while the log held 139
entries. No stated boundary, no note, no check: thirty-eight entries
unreachable from the index that exists to reach them. `todo_index.py`
regenerates the backlog index and has never drifted; the defect index was
maintained by hand and nothing counted the two against each other.

Regenerated from the entry headings. All 119 anchors resolve, verified against
the headings rather than by reading.

## 5. The repair, and the shape of the check

`brief_check.py` normalises each language's spelling of a number, removes
dates, clock times and version strings, then compares every remaining figure
between the two files **by value and by count**, and twenty-five pinned figures
against `STATUS.json` with no floor under any of them. The count half matters:
a figure quoted twice on one side and once on the other is how a corrected
number ends up standing beside the value it replaced.

It was shown failing before it was allowed to pass. Six deliberate mutations -
a reverted defect count, a reverted coverage figure, a test count differing
between the two languages, two decimals restored to a weekly rate, a date
dropped from one side, a reverted decision count - and six caught. Then it
caught a seventh unprompted: adding the F140 entry moved `defects_logged` from
118 to 119 and the briefs still said 118.

**Three errors of the same class were made writing it**, all by reasoning about
the shape of the data instead of reading it: a date pattern assuming a single
space, which a line wrap defeats; a trailing year left standing after the day
and month were stripped, which read as a quantity appearing four times on one
side and once on the other; and a version pattern matching `wersja` while the
Polish prose inflects to `wersji`. All three were found by running the module
against the files. None would have been found by reading it.

## 6. What was corrected that no check will hold

Stated plainly, because the repair above could otherwise read as complete.

Both briefs described the public map as running at a temporary address whose
permanent replacement did not exist, three weeks after `mavo.org.pl` went
public and eleven days after the operator read that page against the channel
through a live raid. The README had said so throughout. Both briefs also stated
that not one conversation had happened with anybody who would receive a
warning. That was untrue, and the correction is a division rather than a
substitution: there is no notification channel, no written record of the
conversation that did happen, and no answer yet to the question that closes
T11, which is the firing rate at which a recipient stops reading.
`docs/FOUNDATIONS.md` carried the 0-of-20 classifier result as the only product
measurement on real data, superseded at 0.22.0.0 by F90, and described the
Telegram channel as the fallback for the APIs eleven days after it went silent
for thirty-four hours and lost primacy to one of them.

Every one of those was found by a person reading. **No check proposed in this
release would have caught any of them**, and the F140 entry says so in those
words rather than closing on the half that was mechanisable.

## 7. Precision, at the reader and at the worksheet

The briefs quoted 5.73 western alert episodes a week and 1.56 western-wide,
which are 81 and 22 events over 99 nights. Two decimals on a count of 22
describes noise. They now read 5.7 and 1.6 with the counts and the window
beside them, and the full quotients stay in `docs/CHANNEL.md`, where the reader
is checking the arithmetic. `precision_lint` cannot reach this: it counts
decimals per file against a ratchet and cannot tell which class a figure
belongs to, which its own docstring says.

Two ceilings rose, both for coverage pins quoted as evidence inside F140, with
the reason written beside each. `README.md`'s has now moved 23, 22, 23, 20, 21
across five releases without a single new claim of false precision, because it
tracks how many places quote a pin whose own formatting changes. The 0.43.0.0
review named the durable repair and this is the third recurrence since.

## 8. Errors of this session

The release number was chosen from the shape of semantic versioning rather than
from `CHANGELOG.md`, which records 0.48.0.1, 0.40.0.1 and 0.39.0.1 as
*documents only* and settles the convention in one grep. The wrong number was
written into three places and shipped for review as a prepared artefact,
labelled as an assumption. A label is not a check, which is the sentence this
release is otherwise about.

The two figure-parity errors and the anchor validator are recorded in section 5.
The pattern across all four is the one both this review and the last one are
about: writing a rule from the shape a thing is assumed to have, rather than
from the shape it has on disk.
