# Review 0.58.0.0: the past week as a file, and three functions that ignored their moment

Version: 1.0 / 2026-09-27

Mandated by D-021 on the major bump. Against `main` at 0.57.0.0, cloned from
GitHub at `a3e9999` in the session, so the base is the remote and not an
upload. **Written by the session that built the release, not by an
independent reviewer**; what stands in for independence is the agreement test
described in section 4, whose oracle is the live code and not this session's
reading of it. `[measured]` means executed in the session's container;
`[measured on the host]` means printed on `vm-mavo` by a command in the
session's runbook, run by the operator; `[reported]` means taken from the
operator's record.

## 1. What was asked

The operator ordered the seven-day slider (P0) ahead of any work on a mobile
application, from the plan of 2026-09-27 (`MOBILE-PLAN-draft.md`, version 0.2
in the Project). On the same day he took the plan's four open decisions: one
file of intervals rather than day files or a range query (D-054's question,
answered by D-058); a slider step of fifteen minutes, against the plan's five;
the reader-facing name "Cofnij mapę" / "Rewind"; and no link to a past moment
in the first version. Only the first binds this repository. The step, the
name and the link belong to the consumer's release (D-S92); the step is
recorded in D-058 because it explains why the file carries intervals no step
rounds.

## 2. The defect the feature found

`poland.warnings_blocks` and `poland.airspace_block` took `as_of` and read the
newest poll, read and list in the table whatever it was. The plan's own check
found that by reading the bodies instead of the signatures. Building the
slider found the same shape a module over: `liveness.measure_feed` read the
newest attempt and read too, so every past moment reported every pipe as
`delivering`, because the age of a read made after the moment is negative and
the function floors an age at zero. Logged as F188. Nothing a reader saw was
wrong, because every caller passed now; that is also why no test could have
told these functions from correct ones.

The repair gives the four store readers an `at` bound (`newest_snapshot` had
one since 0.55.0.0 and no caller used it) and takes the rules out of the reads,
as `warnings_verdict` and `airspace_reading`, so the live cycle and the
timeline apply the same functions to rows fetched two ways.

## 3. What was built

`mavo/timeline.py` and `timeline.json`, specified in D-058 and in the rows of
`docs/WEBAPP.md`. The choice worth a reviewer's time is the clock. An area's
picture of a moment is `compose` over the events the source stamped at or
before it, which is what the store knows today, including rows that reached it
after an outage; the alternative, what the page showed at that moment, would
replay the instrument's blindness as if it were the sky, and the blindness is
the `coverage` layer's to say. The Polish lists have no clock but the read, so
they change at read time and lag the publisher by up to one poll. The two
choices are stated in the module's docstring, in D-058 and in the changelog,
because a consumer that assumed one clock for all four layers would draw a
communique a quarter of an hour early.

Around it: `mavo report --timeline` and `--timeline-every`; `mavo timeline`
(`--out` for T90, `--store --at` and `--file --at` for the acceptance diff on
the host); `mavo record-week`; `report.area_picture` and `report.area_item`,
taken out of `compose` and `to_contract` so the file and the contract name an
area with one function; and `tools/contract_check.py`'s `check_timeline`,
which holds the areas the file carries at its own moment equal to the
contract's `areas` block composed then.

**One change reaches the live cycle, and it is deliberate.** A full tie between
two kinds of one area (same state, same source stamp, same ingest stamp, which
is one API poll reporting two threats that began in the same second) was named
by whichever kind the fold met first. The fold and the timeline meet rows in
different orders, so the headline is now the kind's name first. No reading of
the store has been made for how often this happens; it is named in the
changelog so an operator who sees a headline change knows why.

## 4. How the file is held to the rules

The test is the release's real safety argument, so its limits are stated as
carefully as its reach.

**The oracle is the live code.** For any moment, `picture_from_store` asks
`compose`, the two composers and `measure_feed` directly;
`picture_from_file` only selects intervals. The two must be equal at random
instants and at sampled boundaries, one microsecond either side. A bug in a
live rule is not caught by this, by construction: the timeline is meant to
agree with the rules, and the rules have their own tests.

**Three kinds of input.** Generated Ukrainian logs (three seeds, with ties on
the source stamp, echoes at the same stamp with another ingest stamp, rows
before the window and after the file's end); the RSO page recorded on
2026-09-16 and the UUP plan of 2026-09-14, served into a real store in the
collectors' order with a refused stretch past the communique ceiling, an alert
whose all-clear is withdrawn so it runs to its `valid_to`, and a structure
dropped from the plan and returned; and a week recorded from the production
store (section 5), at about two hundred instants with boundaries drawn per
layer.

**Nine mutations made by hand** `[measured]`: expiries dropped from the change
points, the communique ceiling's edge dropped, the fold's ingest order
ignored, the coverage edge without its tick, reservation edges dropped, the
composer reading the newest list, a full tie decided by order, liveness
reading the tail, the read at the window's start ignored. The generated and
recorded-fixture tests kill all nine. The first version of the generator did
not: it almost never produced two rows of one key at one source stamp, and the
mutation that ignores ingest order survived until echoes were added. The
production week alone kills five; the four it cannot kill are situations the
week does not contain, and they are named rather than assumed away: no RSO
stretch past the ceiling, no rows tied on the source stamp, no full tie, and
no Polish read before the window's start, since the Polish record begins
inside it.

## 5. The recorded week, and the recorder's first error

`mavo/recorder.py` copies, from a store, the rows the week's check reads:
per `(area_id, kind)` the rows at the newest source stamp at or before the
window's start, every event inside it, the two lists the timeline reads with
the rows they name, and per pipe the first poll and the newest poll, read and
refusal at or before the start, then every poll inside. It imports nothing
from the package, so the host's own interpreter ran it before this release
existed.

**Its first version copied the lists of all five RSO addresses**, against its
own docstring, which promised the rows the check reads and nothing else. The
recording came out at 54,489,088 B with 46,542 communiques `[measured on the
host]`, most of them water levels and road notices the timeline never reads.
Caught by reading the host's own row counts before the file was asked for, and
fixed with a test that holds the recorder's two addresses equal to
`poland.WARNINGS_URL` and `pansa.SOURCE_URL`. The second recording is
13,017,088 B with 81 communiques and 147 lists `[measured on the host]`, and is
the fixture: 1,350,168 B after `xz -9e`, its digest checked against the host's
`sha256sum` before anything else runs. One further run executed the first
version a second time, because the download kept the old file's name; its
output was byte for byte the first, which is how it showed, and the second
version was sent again under its own name.

**Before the fixture was committed, every text column was read** for
addresses, credentials, paths and host names, and none was found; the polls'
URLs are the public endpoints, and the only free text is timeouts and the
reconcile unit's fixed reason `[measured]`.

**The recording was taken as root, and that was checked rather than
assumed.** A read-only open of a WAL store whose `-wal` and `-shm` are absent
creates them, and a pair created by root would leave the collectors, which run
as `mavo`, unable to write. SQLite gives the files it creates the database's
owner when it runs as root: reproduced in the container with SQLite 3.45.1, a
root read-only open followed by a write as the owner `[measured]`, and on the
host the store read `mavo:mavo`, with no `-wal` or `-shm` and `state.json`
eight seconds old at 18:34:56 UTC `[measured on the host]`. T90's runbook runs
the release's code as `mavo` all the same.

## 6. What the recorded week says about cost

For the week ending 2026-09-26 the file is 1,143,022 B as written and 113,149 B
gzipped; with the other files' indentation it would be 1,542,590 B, which is
why this one is written compact. The 3,753 area intervals are nine tenths of
it. Built in 1.79 s in the container from 7,491 events `[measured]`. On the
host the replay is the whole log, 42,353 events on 2026-09-27 `[measured on the
host]`, so the cycle's cost there is T90's and is not extrapolated here. At
every cycle the push would carry the file every thirty seconds, about 3.3 GB a
day `[inference]`; `--timeline-every` exists so that figure can be divided
without a release.

## 7. What this release does not carry

- The consumer's half: the `timeline` target in `deploy/accept-state`, the
  `/timeline.json` route and the slider itself are `mavo-site` 4.88.0.0 under
  D-S92, whose tree this session did not have.
- The switch-on: no unit passes `--timeline`. The order is in
  `docs/DEPLOYMENT.md`, and T90 comes before the flag.
- The host's cycle cost, and so the value of `--timeline-every`.
- The growth the host reading measured: `communiques` holds 59,516 rows since
  2026-09-19, almost all from the categories the map does not paint
  `[measured on the host]`. It is D-054's retention question and belongs to
  its own decision.

## 8. Gate

`make verify` green in the container on CPython 3.11.15: 1093 tests, coverage
96.45% against the floor of 95, every audit holding, in 75 s where 0.57.0.0
took 36 s in the same container `[measured]`. The difference is the
timeline's two test files, 44 s of the run under coverage, 21 s of it the
recorded week at some two hundred instants. It is the release's safety
argument and it is kept; the cheaper oracle, folding without `compose`'s
windows, would be a second copy of the fold, which is the thing the test
exists to rule out.

**T90 after the tag, not before it.** The plan put the measurement before the
tag. The only thing it can decide is `--timeline-every`, a flag already in
this release, and without `--timeline` in the unit nothing builds the file; so
the measurement moves to the installed release, before the flag, where it
costs no second patch.
