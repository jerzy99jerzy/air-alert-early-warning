## 2. What was built

`pl_rcb_recent`, one key of `state.json`, composed on every cycle under its
own guard, and `mavo/rcb_recent.py`, which composes it. It answers three
questions from two sources.

**What painted** comes from `timeline.rcb_layer`, which already asks
`poland.warnings_verdict` at every instant the communique verdict can change,
for the slider. The module takes those intervals over forty-eight hours and
regroups them per voivodeship and per communique, and decides nothing about
them.

**Why a painting stopped, and which all-clears RCB announced**, are read off
the lists the store recorded, version by version, with the composer's own
readings of a row: `classify`, `_issued_at`, `expiry_instant`. The verdict
cannot answer these, because it drops a communique whose `valid_to` has
passed, and RCB both shortens and lengthens `valid_to` while a communique
stands. The five reasons each carry `clock`: `rcb` where `at` is a stamp RCB
wrote (an all-clear's `announced`, a rewrite's `updated_at`, a `valid_to`),
`read` where it is a read of this pipe (a withdrawal, an end nobody read).
An all-clear carries `announced`, RCB's `updated_at` for its first version
that is an all-clear, and it is one of the window's events when that falls
inside the window.

**What the reads could not cover** is `unread`: two reads further apart than
the RSO pipe's own outage threshold, the verdict's `null` stretches, and the
stretch before the first poll. The threshold is read from the table
`liveness` judges the pipe by.

Around it: a field on `Report`, a parameter through `compose` and `publish`,
the guard in the report loop, the contract writer's one branch, `reads_over`
and `lists_over` made public in `mavo/timeline.py` for the second reader, rows
in `docs/WEBAPP.md` and `check_rcb_recent` in `tools/contract_check.py`, built
from the recorded page of 2026-09-16 without a store.

reviewer tried. Everything else is F194 and F193:

## 5. What the recording said about RCB

Eight alerts in the week, ended three by a rewrite of the alert into its own
all-clear, three by `valid_to`, one by a separate all-clear swapped in at a
single read, one gone with no all-clear, first read gone 4.5 minutes before
its `valid_to` `[measured]`. D-055 pairs an alert and an all-clear inside one
list, and RCB never listed the two together, so the pairing ended none of
them; the map was right each time because each alert stopped painting on its
own terms: rewritten into an all-clear, past its `valid_to`, or gone. RCB
moves `valid_to` while a communique stands, later on an alert and earlier on
an all-clear, which is one of D-055's reopen conditions, and it moves
`updated_at` on a rewrite, 3.7 to 9.5 minutes before the read that saw each
`[measured]`. None of this changes D-055 here. It is T86's evidence, and T86
is the reading that decides.

## 6. What it does not do

It does not see an alert raised and taken off the list between two reads, and
an empty `unread` does not say otherwise; an alert raised and rewritten
between two reads appears as its all-clear alone, dated by RCB's stamp. It
does not carry the communique's text or any count, and it publishes no
duration: the stamps and the stretches are there, and a duration would be a
figure computed here that nothing asked for. It does not know the host's
cost, which T92 measures: in the session's container one composition over
the recorded two days took 0.118 to 0.123 s over five runs, and the key is
2,705 B compact and adds 3,913 B to `state.json` as written `[measured]`.
