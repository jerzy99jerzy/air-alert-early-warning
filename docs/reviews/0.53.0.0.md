# Review 0.53.0.0: the pipeline learns to say which source it lost

Version: 1.0 / 2026-09-08

Mandated as "we need a notification when the API dies without our knowledge for
about an hour, and then a pop-up has to appear automatically", against the
0.52.1.0 producer tree and the 4.65.4.0 consumer tree as uploaded on
2026-09-08, and against the production host `vm-mavo` read live in the same
session. `[measured]` means read from the machine or executed in the container
this session; `[reported]` means taken from a document and not re-measured
here. Depth is stated per area rather than implied.

## 1. The request was already half-implemented, and the half that existed was the wrong half

`render.py:972` on the deployed consumer raises `state-no-source` at
`observation_age_s > valid_for_s * 6`, which at the producer's packaged 600 s
is exactly the hour the request named, and `outageSync()` opens the modal from
the class the bar carries. So an automatic pop-up after an hour of nothing has
shipped since 4.65.4.0 `[measured, tree]`.

What had not shipped is any connection between that pop-up and the health of a
named pipe. The trigger reads a global maximum, and the whole of this release
is the argument that a global maximum cannot answer the question.

## 2. What the production store said

Seven reads on `vm-mavo`, all read-only, none touching the network
`[measured, 2026-09-08 15:03 to 16:09 UTC]`.

**The channel is not what the handover said it was.** The incoming handover
recorded the Telegram channel as dead since 2026-08-29 04:55:28, confirmed by
three independent third-party readings. The store disagrees in a way that
matters: `telegram` produced 1 141 rows in the preceding 72 h with an ingest
lag between 3.7 s and 33 s, which is live reading and not backfill. Per-day
counts settle it - the channel stopped on 08-29, produced nothing on 08-30,
08-31 and 09-01, **resumed on 2026-09-02 at 13:48:04** after a 377 555 s
silence, ran for five days, and stopped again on **2026-09-07 06:09:04**.

The handover's fact was correct on the day it was taken and had gone stale by
the day it was written. That is a different failure from a wrong measurement
and a more common one. The reviewer's first correction overstated it as
external observers outvoting the project's own instrument, and that overstate-
ment is logged here rather than quietly dropped.

**The consequence, which was live during the review.** At the moment of
reading, one of two feeds had produced nothing for 33 h 50 min while its own
pipe recorded 7 852 attempts and 7 852 successful reads, and the contract said
`ok` throughout. The failure this release repairs was not hypothetical and was
not historical; it was the current state of production.

**Two silences that must not read alike.** On the evening of 2026-09-06 both
feeds went 4 958 s and then 4 922 s without an observation, beginning inside
the same minute, with both pipes at full health. Any threshold on observation
age at one hour raises there. Daily row counts run in step across the window -
949 against 904, 883 against 877, 751 against 755 - which is what two delivery
paths of one origin look like and is why one cannot cover for the other.

**Refusals are recorded, classifiable, and rarer than assumed.** Nine in 1 296
attempts over 72 h: five consecutive 503 spanning about ten minutes on 09-06,
which is a vendor outage nobody knew had happened; one isolated 502; two 401 on
09-07; one 401 on 09-08 on the first poll after the timer was restarted.
**No 403 at any point.** The Cloudflare 1010 with `browser_signature_banned`
in the incident record was served to hand-made diagnostic requests and never to
the collector, so the collector's failure was rejected authentication and not a
ban, and the two had been folded into one story. The 401 recurring after the
block was lifted, with 1 287 successful reads on either side of it, also lifts
the standing suspicion from `/etc/mavo/ukrainealarm.key`: a corrupted key file
produces a persistent 401, not an intermittent one.

## 3. The threshold, and the constant that was designed and then deleted

The consumer's own comment at `SOURCE_SILENCE_IS_AN_OUTAGE_BEATS` names what
would move it: a measured distribution of gaps on the API feed. This session
produced one. Of the nine gaps above 3 600 s in fourteen days, seven are
artefacts - one predates D-040, four fall inside the outage window and are the
shape of partial recovery rather than quiet, and two are the switchover day.
The two that remain are the pair on 09-06. In clean operation there is **no gap
above 5 400 s**, so the deployed 3 600 s carries roughly one false claim every
four or five days, and the mechanism is not tunable: `ts_source` on this feed
is the alarm's own `began`, so an evening in which nothing new *starts* holds
the maximum still while fifty alarms run and every poll returns 200.

A second constant counting consecutive refusals was specified, and then removed
before it was written. It is redundant: the n-th refusal in a row is n cadences
without a successful read, so `cadence_s * GAP_CADENCES` already carries it.
Checked against all four refusal episodes in the table, the single rule fires
on the second consecutive 503 of 09-06 and on the second 401 of 09-07 - ninety
seconds before an operator stopped that timer by hand - and stays silent on
both isolated refusals. Two constants became one with strictly better
behaviour, which is worth recording because the reverse is the usual direction.

## 4. What this release ships, and what it deliberately does not

Ships: `mavo/liveness.py`, three indexed tail accessors on `EventStore`, a
`sources` block on `state.json` additive against schema v3, and the per-source
stamp fold inside the pass `compose` already makes. 691 tests, 95.46%,
`make verify` green `[measured]`.

Does not ship, and the omission is the decision: the observation-age heuristic
on the consumer is untouched. A new signal is added beside a working one, and
the old one is loosened only after the new one has been observed correct on the
host. Swapping a working detector for an unproven one inside one release is how
a page loses a check it had.

Also does not ship: any operator notification. The pop-up is for a reader. The
question of how a human learns about an outage without opening the site is
open, and the architecture note this review can offer is that `vm-site` already
polls `state.json` from a second host, so once the block lands there is an
off-host observer that does not die with `vm-mavo`. What is missing is a path
from its knowledge to a phone, and that is a decision about a third party or a
self-hosted receiver rather than an engineering gap.

## 5. Where the review looked, and how hard

Deep, line-level: `report.py` (`Report`, `compose`, `to_contract`, `publish`),
`store.py` (`feed_attempts` schema, `_record`, `attempts`, `iter_attempts`,
`newest_page_id`), `attempts.py` in full, `cli.py` around `reconcile`,
`collect-api` and the `publish` call site, and on the consumer `render.py`
(`headline`, `outage_dialog`, the outage script) and `contract.py`
(`validate`, `REQUIRED_TOP`, `FEED_STATES`).

Read to answer one question each: `sources/telegram.py` (`CHANNEL_URL`,
`source_id`), `sources/ukrainealarm_source.py` (`source_id`, the CLEAR-by-
absence path), `schema.py` (`ThreatKind` members, read after the reviewer
guessed one and a test caught it).

**Not read at line level, and named so it is not mistaken for reviewed:**
`areas.py`, `kinds.py`, `evaluate.py`, `policy.py`, `rules.py`, `baserate.py`,
`backfill.py`, `obs.py`, `transport.py`, `sources/rso.py`, every tool under
`tools/` other than `docs_audit.py` where the gate refused, and the whole of
the consumer outside the two files above. The mutation register was not run in
the container; the producer gate's `harness-mutation` target was.
